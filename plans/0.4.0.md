# 0.4.0: architectural dimensions

Status: planned.

ArchiScript 0.3.x establishes VDPs, operations, sequential composition, and the
symmetric monoidal tensor for independent semantic dimensions. Version 0.4.0
should explore a reusable library of **software-architecture problem
dimensions** built on that foundation.

The goal is not to add technology catalogs such as AWS, Kubernetes, Kafka,
PostgreSQL, or CLI products to the calculus. Those are implementations and
mechanisms. The reusable layer should instead model architectural problems that
occur across technologies and domains.

Examples include time, failure, availability, concurrency, distribution,
communication, consistency, resource pressure, lifecycle, ownership, trust,
policy, durability, and observability.

## Design principle

A candidate belongs in this layer when it describes an architectural concern
that can be stated independently of a particular product or implementation
mechanism.

Good candidates:

- time and deadline position;
- failure and degradation;
- availability;
- concurrency and contention;
- distribution and reachability;
- communication and delivery state;
- consistency;
- resource pressure and capacity;
- lifecycle;
- ownership;
- trust and authorization posture;
- policy applicability;
- durability;
- observability and knowledge of state;
- topology and version compatibility where they prove generally useful.

Poor foundational candidates:

- Kafka;
- PostgreSQL;
- Kubernetes;
- AWS Lambda;
- SQS;
- circuit breaker;
- retry library;
- cache;
- load balancer;
- autoscaler.

The latter may later be modeled as profiles, examples, or library compositions
of more general architectural dimensions.

For example:

```text
queue
  = Communication
  + Capacity
  + Time

cache
  = Consistency
  + Time
  + Resources

database transaction
  = Concurrency
  + Consistency
  + Durability

circuit breaker
  = Failure
  + Availability
  + Time
```

These equalities are explanatory decompositions, not new equations in the
ArchiScript calculus.

## Tensor as architectural context

Many concerns are naturally represented as independent VDPs and combined with
a business VDP only where an operation depends on them.

For example:

```text
PaymentState
  = pending | accepted | rejected

Deadline
  = beforeDeadline | expired

Failure
  = healthy | degraded | failed
```

An operation whose architectural decision depends on all three may use the full
product:

```text
PaymentState ⊗ Deadline ⊗ Failure
```

This exposes combinations that ordinary happy-path diagrams often omit, such
as:

```text
pending  × expired        × healthy
accepted × expired        × degraded
pending  × beforeDeadline × failed
```

The tensor retains its 0.3.x meaning: it is the full independent carrier and
member product. It does not itself assert runtime parallelism, causal
independence, resource effects, reachability, or that every product member can
occur in a particular implementation.

Architectural relevance is local. A model should not tensor every known
dimension into every operation. Each operation should depend only on the
dimensions needed to state its architectural decision.

Conceptually:

```text
global library of dimensions
        |
        +-- Time
        +-- Failure
        +-- Availability
        +-- Consistency
        +-- ...
        |
        v
operation-local product
BusinessState ⊗ Time ⊗ Failure
```

This avoids treating the global Cartesian product as the architecture and limits
combinatorial explosion.

## Candidate problem dimensions

### Time

Represents architecture-relevant classifications of time rather than a clock
implementation.

Possible uses include:

- before / at / after a deadline;
- fresh / stale / expired;
- lease validity;
- retention windows;
- bounded age.

A timeout threshold may therefore be expressed as an ordinary semantic
partition over a suitable carrier such as elapsed duration.

```text
Elapsed
├── beforeTimeout
└── timedOut
```

This can be tensored with a business VDP when the business decision depends on
timeout position.

### Failure

Represents the architectural distinction between normal, degraded, recoverable,
and terminal conditions.

It should describe failure semantics, not a particular recovery mechanism.

### Availability

Represents whether a capability required by another architectural decision is
available, degraded, or unavailable.

Availability is distinct from failure: a component may be healthy but
unreachable, or degraded while still providing the capability needed by the
consumer.

### Concurrency

Represents architecture-relevant simultaneous-access or contention classes when
those classes can be stated as ordinary semantic state.

Examples may include exclusive, shared, or contended ownership states.

This dimension must not claim that tensor itself proves races, ordering, or
interleaving behavior. Such conclusions require stronger resource/effect or
execution semantics.

### Distribution

Represents facts introduced by independently located or independently failing
participants, such as membership, reachability, partitioning, replica role, or
quorum position.

The module should remain independent of any particular distributed platform.

### Communication

Represents architecture-relevant states of information transfer, such as
pending, delivered, duplicated, rejected, or unavailable delivery.

It should not bake in one transport such as HTTP, Kafka, queues, or RPC.

### Consistency

Represents relationships between independently observable versions or views of
state, such as current, stale, conflicting, or unresolved.

Exact consistency guarantees require explicit modeled assumptions. Names alone
must never imply stronger distributed-system guarantees.

### Resources

Represents finite resource conditions relevant to architecture decisions, for
example available, constrained, or exhausted.

The dimension may cover abstract resources before introducing specializations
for CPU, memory, connections, storage, or quotas.

### Capacity

Represents demand relative to available capacity, for example nominal,
pressured, saturated, or overloaded.

Capacity should remain distinct from the resource itself: resource state and
demand state may vary independently.

### Lifecycle

Represents architectural phases in the existence of a modeled entity, such as
creating, active, retiring, or gone.

It should remain generic enough to describe data, services, deployments,
sessions, leases, or other entities without becoming a deployment-specific
ontology.

### Ownership

Represents which architectural participant, if any, has authority or exclusive
responsibility for a resource or state transition.

This may be useful for locks, leases, leaders, writers, or responsibility
boundaries without making those mechanisms foundational.

### Trust

Represents whether an architectural boundary treats information or an actor as
trusted, untrusted, authenticated, challenged, or otherwise admissible under an
explicit model.

Concrete identity protocols and credential systems belong outside the
foundation.

### Policy

Represents whether an architectural action or state is allowed, forbidden,
conditional, or exceptional under explicitly declared policy.

It should model architectural constraints rather than encode a particular
policy engine.

### Durability

Represents the persistence guarantees assumed for state, for example volatile,
persisted, or redundantly persisted.

As with consistency, the selected names are meaningful only through explicitly
declared semantics and assumptions.

### Observability

Represents what the architecture knows about another semantic state.

This is particularly important because:

```text
the system is healthy
```

and

```text
the observer knows the system is healthy
```

are different claims.

The Stage 2 backlog already identifies observer-relative support sets and
observation narrowing as future work. Any 0.4.0 observability work must preserve
that distinction rather than redefining VDP membership.

### Possible secondary dimensions

Topology and version compatibility are plausible reusable dimensions, but they
should be added only if concrete architecture models demonstrate that they are
not better expressed by the primary dimensions above.

## What 0.4.0 should not claim

A VDP can classify a current architecture-relevant state. Tensor can combine
independent classifications. This covers more architectural concerns than a
business-only model, but it does not turn the current calculus into a temporal
or distributed model checker.

In particular, these claims are not justified merely by adding more VDPs:

- every request eventually completes;
- a timeout action happens within one minute;
- a retry occurs only after a required backoff;
- two concurrent executions cannot race;
- messages are globally ordered;
- consensus eventually elects a leader;
- a distributed write is linearizable;
- a production deployment conforms to the model.

For example:

```text
elapsed < 60 s
elapsed >= 60 s
```

is naturally a VDP.

But:

```text
after entering Pending, the system must reach Completed or TimedOut
within 60 seconds
```

is a statement about an execution history, not only a current-state
classification.

## Deferred behavioral layer

If ArchiScript later needs to prove ordering, history, bounded eventuality,
fairness, interleavings, or other temporal properties, that work should add a
separate behavioral layer rather than overloading tensor.

A possible direction is:

```text
Domain
  ↓
VDP
  ↓
Operation
  ↓
Transition system / execution
  ↓
Trace properties
```

Potential property classes include invariants, reachability, eventuality,
bounded eventuality, and until-like properties.

This is intentionally not part of the 0.4.0 commitment unless a minimal design
can be added without changing the established meaning of VDP, Operation,
composition, or tensor.

## Library shape

The architectural-dimension layer should be additive and built from the public
calculus wherever possible.

A possible organization is:

```text
ArchiScript/
  ... core calculus ...

ArchiScriptArchitecture/
  Time
  Failure
  Availability
  Concurrency
  Distribution
  Communication
  Consistency
  Resources
  Capacity
  Lifecycle
  Ownership
  Trust
  Policy
  Durability
  Observability
```

The exact package/module boundary remains open. The important constraint is that
these dimensions do not silently become new foundational primitives merely
because they are reusable.

Concrete technology profiles may later live above this layer:

```text
technology or mechanism
        ↓ interpreted as
architectural dimensions
        ↓ built from
VDP + Operation + comp + tensor
```

Such profiles must not make product names part of the mathematical foundation.

## 0.4.0 acceptance direction

Before treating any dimension as reusable library API, provide at least one
nontrivial example showing that:

1. the dimension describes a software-architecture problem rather than a
   particular product;
2. its carrier and members have explicit semantics;
3. tensor with an independent business VDP preserves the full product;
4. an operation can depend on the dimension without forcing unrelated
   dimensions into its domain;
5. the model reveals a meaningful architectural case that was hidden in the
   business-only model;
6. the example does not overclaim temporal, concurrency, runtime, or production
   guarantees unsupported by the calculus.

The first target should be a small set of dimensions that exercise clearly
different problems rather than implementing the entire candidate list at once.
Time, Failure, Communication, Consistency, and Resources are strong initial
candidates because together they test independent context, degraded operation,
information transfer, state relationships, and finite capacity while remaining
technology-neutral.

0.4.0 should therefore be judged by whether ArchiScript can express richer
software-architecture questions while keeping the core calculus small and the
meaning of tensor precise—not by the number of infrastructure concepts added.
