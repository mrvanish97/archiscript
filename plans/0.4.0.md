# 0.4.0: semantic structure beyond independent tensor

Status: planned.

ArchiScript 0.3.x establishes strict Value-Domain Partitions (VDPs), ordinary
partial-member Operations, sequential composition, and the symmetric monoidal
tensor for independent semantic coordinates.

Version 0.4.0 should extend that calculus without introducing a standardized
ontology of software architecture. ArchiScript should not define what "time",
"failure", "availability", "consistency", "ownership", or similar concepts mean.
Those meanings belong to user-defined value domains, subdomains, VDPs, and
operations.

The objective is instead to strengthen the generic mathematical relationships
between VDPs that are already present in the ArchiScript design vocabulary.

The primary candidates are:

- refinement and coarsening;
- common refinement for multiple semantic views of one carrier;
- member-dependent refinement;
- formal semantics of semantic superposition and observation.

These should remain distinct from independent tensor, PVDP parameterization,
ordinary operation composition, and external appearances.

## Design principle

ArchiScript standardizes the form of architectural reasoning, not the contents
of the architecture's vocabulary.

A user may choose to model:

- elapsed duration;
- logical clocks;
- retries;
- queue state;
- authorization;
- replica state;
- resource pressure;
- deployment phases;
- business decisions;

or none of those.

The foundation should require only that the relevant carriers, subdomains,
partitions, mappings, and evidence are stated precisely enough for the selected
proof obligations.

For example, ArchiScript should not provide a foundational Time VDP such as:

    Time
    ├── beforeDeadline
    └── expired

Instead, a user may define any carrier and VDP whose semantics fit the model.
The calculus should then be able to relate that VDP to other VDPs without
knowing that the user's interpretation happens to concern time.

## Existing vocabulary remains authoritative

0.4.0 must preserve the distinctions already established in the mathematical
foundation and Stage 2 design.

### Value subdomain

A value subdomain is a semantic subset of an existing value domain.

Subdomains may overlap and need not be exhaustive. Their formulas, when
available, define their extensions. Opaque subdomains remain valid when the
extension is intentionally unavailable to the analyzer.

### Value-Domain Partition

A VDP partitions one nonempty carrier into a finite family of nonempty,
exhaustive, pairwise-disjoint semantic members.

VDP membership remains extensional semantic truth. An occurrence belongs to
exactly one fully determined member.

### Operation

An Operation is a partial function between finite VDP member families:

    M_X ⇀ M_Y

It is not a runtime implementation, effect, trace, transport, or value-level
function.

### Composition

Sequential operation composition retains its existing right-to-left meaning:

    X --f--> Y --g--> Z

    g.comp f

Sequential order belongs to composition, not tensor.

### Independent tensor

The tensor:

    P ⊗ Q

continues to mean full independent aggregation.

Its carrier is the full Cartesian product:

    Carrier(P) × Carrier(Q)

and its members are the full Cartesian product of the factor members.

Tensor does not:

- prune combinations;
- imply shared identity;
- imply runtime concurrency;
- establish causality;
- establish compatibility;
- model one value through two semantic views;
- express resource or effect semantics.

Operation tensor remains the independent product of partial member mappings.

### PVDP parameterization

A PVDP parameter retains its Stage 2 meaning: changing the parameter member must
change which outbound operations exist from at least one specialization.

A runtime or environmental value that merely changes the result of the same
operation graph belongs in ordinary carrier data or an ordinary VDP factor, not
in a PVDP parameter.

### Appearance

An appearance remains an external boundary axiom stating that members of a VDP
may be supplied without a modeled producer.

It is not an Operation and must not be repurposed as a generic initialization,
observation, or execution primitive.

## 1. Refinement and coarsening

The mathematical foundation already defines a refinement order for VDPs on the
same carrier.

For VDPs P and Q over the same carrier:

    P ≼ Q

means that P is finer than Q, and Q is a coarsening of P.

Every member of P is contained in exactly one member of Q.

The existing quotient characterization should become a first-class checked Lean
API:

    q[P,Q] : Members(P) → Members(Q)

with the expected properties:

- totality;
- surjectivity;
- uniqueness for the declared refinement relation;
- compatibility with the two classifiers.

Conceptually:

    fine semantic resolution
            |
            | quotient
            v
    coarse semantic resolution

This construction is intentionally domain-neutral.

For example, a user may define:

    P = { A1, A2, A3, A4 }
    Q = { B1, B2 }

and prove P ≼ Q without ArchiScript assigning any architectural meaning to the
member names.

### Why this is high leverage

Refinement lets the same carrier be inspected at different semantic resolutions
without inventing intermediate processing steps.

It also gives ArchiScript a rigorous basis for questions such as:

- does one model distinguish strictly more cases than another?
- which fine members collapse into one coarse member?
- can an operation be reviewed at a coarser resolution?
- did a model change erase a distinction that previously existed?

These are architectural questions independent of any particular domain
vocabulary.

## 2. Common refinement

Two VDPs may ask different semantic questions about the same carrier.

This is not tensor.

For VDPs P and Q on one carrier C, their common refinement:

    P ∧ Q

has the same carrier C and members formed from the nonempty intersections of
members of P and Q.

The distinction must remain explicit:

    P ⊗ Q

means two independent carrier coordinates:

    C × C

whereas:

    P ∧ Q

means two semantic views of one coordinate:

    C

0.4.0 should provide a checked construction for common refinement rather than
forcing users to rebuild the intersection partition manually.

### Same-value synchronization

If a model starts from independent coordinates and later requires them to refer
to one and the same value, that requirement must remain explicit.

The monoidal design identifies the diagonal:

    Δ_C = { (x, x) | x ∈ C }

as the relevant same-value relation.

0.4.0 should not redefine tensor to insert this diagonal implicitly.

## 3. Member-dependent refinement

Independent tensor is deliberately complete, but not every useful semantic
decomposition is independent.

For a VDP:

    P : C -> Members(P)

suppose each member M has its own local VDP:

    Q_M : M -> Members(Q_M)

The total semantic refinement then has member family:

    coproduct over M in Members(P) of Members(Q_M)

Conceptually:

    P
    ├── A
    │   ├── A1
    │   └── A2
    ├── B
    │   └── B
    └── C
        ├── C1
        ├── C2
        └── C3

This is the standardized member-dependent refinement already described in the
monoidal design review.

It must remain distinct from tensor:

    P ⊗ Q

would create every independent pair of members from both VDPs.

Member-dependent refinement instead states that the available finer semantic
distinctions depend on the already selected member of P.

### Why this is high leverage

This construction avoids manufacturing impossible Cartesian combinations while
preserving the rule that tensor itself is never pruned.

It also provides a precise answer to models where:

- one successful member needs several finer distinctions;
- one failed member needs no further distinction;
- different parent members admit different semantic decompositions.

The correct response is not a filtered tensor and not automatically a PVDP.
It is a refinement of semantic resolution.

## 4. Semantic superposition and observation

Stage 2 already distinguishes semantic truth from observer knowledge.

A fully determined occurrence belongs to exactly one VDP member.

Before enough evidence is available, an observer may only know that the
occurrence belongs to a nonempty subset of the member family.

This unresolved knowledge is semantic superposition.

0.4.0 should formalize the Stage 2 backlog proposal.

For a VDP P, an observer-relative support should represent a nonempty subset:

    S ⊆ Members(P)
    S ≠ ∅

Observation should narrow support:

    S_before -> S_after

with:

    S_after ⊆ S_before

A complete observation narrows support to a singleton.

At minimum the formalization should establish:

- support is always nonempty;
- observation narrows monotonically;
- repeated identical observation is idempotent;
- complete observation produces singleton support;
- observation does not change the underlying VDP member;
- observation is not an Operation merely because both have arrows in a
  diagram.

### Why this belongs in the foundation

This is not a domain-specific observability model.

It formalizes the already standardized distinction between:

    what semantic state the occurrence actually inhabits

and:

    which semantic states an observer can still consider possible

The same construction can support arbitrary user-defined domains without
introducing Known, Unknown, Healthy, or other prescribed semantic members.

## 5. What does not require a new primitive

0.4.0 should avoid adding names for constructions already expressible in the
current calculus.

### Preserving independent context

Given:

    f : P -> P'

and an independent VDP Q, applying f while preserving Q is already:

    f ⊗ id_Q :
    P ⊗ Q -> P' ⊗ Q

No separate "context lifting" primitive is needed.

### Reassociation and reordering

Associator, unitors, and symmetry already provide the canonical structural
isomorphisms for tensor factors.

No architecture-specific reindexing mechanism should duplicate those laws.

### Joint semantic questions over product carriers

If two values are independent inputs but the architecture asks a joint semantic
question about the pair, define a VDP over the product carrier.

For example:

    Carrier := A × B

    JointQuestion
    ├── Case1
    └── Case2

This is distinct from the tensor of two existing classifications.

The Stage 2 resolution example already uses this distinction:

    SemVerRange × NpmEnv -> ResolutionResult

The product carrier supplies the joint data; the result VDP asks its own
semantic question.

A new foundational generic relation entity is therefore not required merely to
classify pairs.

### Projection terminology

Stage 2 already uses projection for generalized views of parameterized
architectures while preserving hidden specialization correlation.

0.4.0 should not reuse that term for ordinary member maps such as forgetting a
tensor coordinate. If such a map is useful, it remains an ordinary Operation
or a non-foundational helper unless stronger mathematics justifies a distinct
concept.

## 6. Separation from PVDP parameterization

The following cases must remain different.

### Independent semantic coordinate

Use tensor:

    P ⊗ Q

when P and Q are independently available semantic coordinates.

### Different semantic resolution inside a member

Use member-dependent refinement when finer distinctions depend on the selected
member.

### Different outbound architecture topology

Use a PVDP only when the parameter member changes which outbound operations
exist.

This gives the design test:

    same operation topology, different input state
        -> ordinary carrier or VDP factor

    same carrier, finer semantic distinction
        -> refinement

    member-specific finer distinction
        -> member-dependent refinement

    different outbound operation set
        -> PVDP

0.4.0 should include regression examples that keep these cases visibly
separate.

## 7. Deduction before new architecture entities

Stage 2 already warns against inductive overreach: starting from a convenient
subset of successful cases can erase the true carrier and manufacture extra
VDPs, operations, or PVDPs whose only purpose is to recover omitted states.

0.4.0 should preserve that discipline.

Before adding a new partition, parameter, or operation, examples should ask:

1. Is the carrier the complete a priori value universe for this boundary?
2. Can the distinction be stated deductively as subdomains of that carrier?
3. Is another VDP actually needed, or is this a different resolution of the
   same carrier?
4. Is tensor genuinely independent aggregation?
5. Is a PVDP parameter changing topology, or merely data?
6. Is an apparent processing stage actually only observation?

These are modeling checks, not new semantic entities.

## 8. Deferred work

0.4.0 should not add execution traces, temporal logic, resource effects, or
distributed-system guarantees merely to model common software vocabulary.

In particular, the following remain outside the claims of the current
operation calculus:

- eventual completion;
- bounded eventuality;
- runtime ordering;
- race freedom;
- deadlock freedom;
- fairness;
- message-delivery guarantees;
- linearizability;
- implementation conformance;
- production effect execution.

A user may already define VDPs whose members describe domain-specific temporal,
failure, consistency, or distributed states. Lean can check the declared VDP
and Operation obligations.

That must not be confused with proving execution-history properties.

If a future release introduces execution semantics, it should do so as a
separate layer with its own mathematical definition rather than by changing the
meaning of tensor, VDP membership, or observation.

## 9. Proposed implementation order

### Phase 1: refinement/coarsening

Implement and test the existing mathematical refinement order in Lean:

- same-carrier refinement witness;
- canonical member quotient;
- identity and transitivity;
- coarsening checks;
- examples demonstrating multiple semantic resolutions of one carrier.

This is the least ontologically invasive extension because the mathematics is
already part of the foundation.

### Phase 2: common refinement

Implement the checked meet construction for two VDPs on one carrier:

- nonempty member intersections;
- coverage;
- disjointness;
- refinement maps back to both source VDPs;
- regression checks distinguishing meet from tensor.

### Phase 3: member-dependent refinement

Implement the construction described in the monoidal review:

- one local VDP per parent member;
- total member family as a dependent coproduct;
- preservation of coverage and disjointness;
- singleton local refinements where no finer distinction is required;
- examples where tensor would introduce semantically irrelevant combinations.

### Phase 4: observation

Formalize the Stage 2 backlog semantics:

- nonempty observer-relative support;
- narrowing;
- complete observation;
- monotonicity;
- idempotence;
- explicit distinction from state-transforming Operation.

Observation is the most semantically novel part of 0.4.0 and should follow the
partition-structure work rather than precede it.

## 10. Acceptance direction

0.4.0 should be considered successful when the Lean library can express richer
semantic architecture structure without introducing a prescribed software
architecture vocabulary.

At minimum:

1. a user can prove one VDP is a refinement of another VDP on the same carrier;
2. the corresponding coarsening quotient is machine checked;
3. two semantic views of one carrier can be combined by common refinement
   without using tensor;
4. member-dependent refinement can refine different parent members differently;
5. tensor remains the complete independent product with unchanged monoidal
   laws;
6. PVDP parameterization remains restricted to changes in outbound operation
   topology;
7. semantic superposition records observer uncertainty without weakening actual
   VDP disjointness;
8. observation narrows knowledge without being misrepresented as a
   state-transforming Operation;
9. examples demonstrate the difference between deduction, refinement, tensor,
   PVDP specialization, observation, and composition;
10. no foundational API requires users to adopt ArchiScript-defined meanings
    for time, failure, availability, consistency, cloud infrastructure, or any
    other software-architecture domain.

The release should therefore be judged by the precision and compositionality of
the calculus, not by the number of architectural concepts whose names appear in
the library.
